## Document Overview

This document details the physical examination, assessment, and treatment plan for a patient with right wrist de Quervain's tenosynovitis, exacerbated by occupational activities.

## Detailed Sections

### Physical Examination
- **General:** Alert, no acute distress.
- **Right Wrist:** Mild swelling over radial aspect; tenderness at first dorsal compartment (APL/EPB tendons). Positive Finkelstein's test. No erythema or warmth. Mildly reduced grip strength (4+/5) due to pain. Flexion/extension mildly limited. No instability.
- **Neurological:** Sensation intact in median/ulnar/radial distributions. Tinel's and Phalen's negative. Reflexes 2+ symmetric.
- **Vascular:** Pulses palpable, normal capillary refill. [[None-Cite-1, Page-2]](#none-cite-1-page-2)

### Assessment
1. Right wrist de Quervain's tenosynovitis (ICD-10 M65.4) - work-related
2. History of distal radius fracture with hardware, fully healed (Z87.81)
3. Recurrent overuse injury, exacerbated by occupational lifting
4. Carpal tunnel syndrome - history only

This represents an acute exacerbation superimposed on a previously repaired wrist. Mechanism (forceful patient lift with wrist deviation) consistent with work-related tendon strain, not recurrence of fracture. Prior trauma increases susceptibility but does not account for acute onset pattern. [[None-Cite-2, Page-2]](#none-cite-2-page-2)

### Plan
1. **Immobilization:** Thumb spica splint during work hours X 2 weeks, remove for gentle ROM exercises.
2. **Medication:** Naproxen 500 mg BID with food X 10 days, then PRN.
3. **Physical/Occupational Therapy:** Initiate OT twice weekly for tendon gliding, soft tissue mobilization, and ergonomic training (3 weeks).
4. **Work restrictions:** Light duty - no lifting >10 lbs, avoid repetitive wrist deviation or gripping >15 minutes/hour.
5. **Imaging:** X-ray wrist (AP/Lateral) to assess hardware and exclude fracture recurrence - medically necessary given prior ORIF.
6. **Patient education:** Discussed chronic strain risk, splint care, and progressive return to normal duty.
7. **Follow-up:** Re-evaluation in 3 weeks (11/12/25). [[None-Cite-3, Page-2]](#none-cite-3-page-2)

## Summary and Conclusions
The patient presents with work-related de Quervain's tenosynovitis, compounded by a history of distal radius fracture. The treatment plan includes immobilization, medication, physical therapy, work restrictions, imaging, patient education, and a follow-up appointment.
